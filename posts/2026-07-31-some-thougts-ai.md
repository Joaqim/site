NOTE: I picked up usage of _em-dash_ _after_ prevalance of AI;before, I only
used ordinary dash: `-`. I then _stopped_ using it after the collective
acknowledgement of AI-agents' obiqitous usage of it, but as these blog are
explicitly human-written I will continue to use _em-dashes_ in these posts.

As AI becomes more entrenched in software development, I find myself striving
for more creative outputs, not driven by profit or performance as motivation.

For better or for worse, at my current position at work, I'm not motivated by
salary—or even by my performance as a developer; I receive luke-warm active
feedback on ongoing projects, and I have feelings that the Company would work
just as well without me as they are currently. I don't take active ownership of
projects, and have no over-arching documentation or plan, unless I write it
myself.

While it might seem bleak I do believe there is room for growth at my position.

Some of the things I've started doing is writing down human-readable
documentation from my personal knowledge. Currently, I'm passionate about Nix
and NixOS, and I find that a declarative language for active deployments and
continues integration (CI) of software and services is a very powerful tool,
especially now when it is easier than ever to create bespoke applications and
services on-demand.

Though, I find that my being pedantic while not utilizing AI-tools is putting
me on the back-foot; I can spend a very long time on details that only matter
to me, and I often find myself hiding these documentations away, or completely
abandon them when I don't receive the feedback I would want.

At the same time, I can see that as long as people are transparent about their
usage of LLMs in technical documentation or proposals it can lead to good
things; never before has the barrier-of-entry to contribute to open-source
projects—even the Linux kernel—been lower. And people, including myself, are
conflicted; just because some progresses feels "inevitable" doesn't make
proponents of said technology "luddites".

As English is not my mother-language, I believe that I have a certain advantage
over many people that are starting to rely an AI-assistance; English is not my
"emotional language"; it is the language used in movies, TV series, coding,
entertainment and international news. All these things are something that I can
remove myself from at the end of the day—for better or for worse as it relates
to current events. My point is: I have inherently less of an emotional
attachment to text written in English, so there is already a clear divide
between AI-assisted writing and personal texts.

I would not expect my friends, colleagues or family to send me AI-writing
instead of a human written response; I shudder to think of the day when my
nieces or nephews would favour an AI response over an imperfectly written human
response.

While in a workplace with active software development, usage of English is
par-for-the-course, and I find that a certain blurring of the line between the
need for AI-written code and "human code"  is less critical, especially when
motivated by profits. LLMs excel at writing code _and_ documentation, and while
humans are actively adapting to get better products out of AI, I do hope there
is a shift for us to be more critical of AI versus Human Intelligence (HI),
especially when it comes to over-arching documentation for any larger project
that expects development and usage between many different operators and
consumers; while we write AI-agent skills, we should also write text explicitly
for us humans still.

Usage of AI usage in creative writing is another can-of-worms that I won't
bring up here—but as an avid reader, I'm conflicted, and I've yet to read a
published work that has an AI-disclaimer.
